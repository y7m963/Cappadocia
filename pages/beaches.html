TOP 5 beach destinations for 2026

From the powder-white sands of the Maldives to the dramatic volcanic cliffs of Santorini, this year's list spans every kind of coastline imaginable: quiet lagoons perfect for a slow morning swim, wide open surf breaks for anyone chasing waves, and secluded coves that can only really be reached by boat. Each destination made the cut for a completely different reason, but all five share one thing in common: water clear enough that you can still see your own feet standing in it.

What actually makes a beach unforgettable? A few things:

  1. Water clarity matters more than sand color. A beach with visibility down to several meters lets you spot fish, coral, and the occasional sea turtle without ever needing to dive, and it's usually the first thing seasoned beach-hoppers check before booking anywhere.
  2. Fewer crowds. The best beaches on this list are a short boat ride or a longer drive from the nearest town, a small inconvenience that keeps them from turning into a parking lot.
  3. Good food nearby, ideally within walking distance.
  4. Reliable weather matters more than most people plan for. A beach can look absolutely perfect in every photo and still be unusable for half the year because of strong winds or a long rainy season, so it's always worth checking the seasonal patterns first before building an entire trip around just one single destination.

Pro tipps

  1. Pack reef-safe sunscreen see why it actually matters, since the regular kind can quietly
    damage coral reefs within minutes of anyone entering the water, even in small amounts.
  2. Book beachfront accommodation a few months ahead for anywhere on this list, especially between June and September
    , since the best rooms with a direct sea view tend to sell out first, particularly around peak season and any major public holiday weekend of the year.
No two beaches on this list feel remotely the same, and that's exactly the point of putting them together in one place. Some reward an early start and a long, quiet swim before breakfast; others are best experienced from a hammock with a cold drink in hand and absolutely nowhere else to be for the rest of the day. What they all share, despite the differences, is a kind of stillness that's increasingly hard to find anywhere else these days: no traffic, no notifications buzzing, just sand, water, and however much time you've managed to set aside to do absolutely nothing at all. Wherever you end up heading this year, it's worth building in at least one full day with no plan whatsoever, because those tend to be the days you end up remembering most once you're back home and everything else has faded.
